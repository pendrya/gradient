# Frontier-class open-weight LLMs (as of 20 Sep 2026) and fit on 128 GB (1x DGX Spark) / 256 GB (2x Spark)

Scope note: all "as of" claims are dated to the source; anything computed by me (memory footprints, bandwidth-bound tok/s ceilings) is labelled as an estimate in the Inferences subsections. Memory formulas used throughout: FP8 ≈ 1.0 byte/param; NVFP4/MXFP4/Q4 ≈ 0.55–0.6 byte/param (4-bit weights + block scales + non-quantized attention/embeddings); ~3 bpw ≈ 0.40 byte/param. Decode ceiling rule of thumb: 273 GB/s ÷ (active params × bytes/param); real-world single-stream results on Spark land at roughly 50–75% of that ceiling unless speculative decoding is used.

## KQ1. Which open-weight models in Sept 2026 are within striking distance of closed frontier models, and what are their sizes?

### Takeaway
As of early Sept 2026 the two best open models (GLM-5.3 at 744B-A40B and Kimi K3 at 2.8T-A104B) score 44–45 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index vs 53 for Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 — a 9-point gap that Interconnects describes as a 3–5-month lag. The "striking-distance" tier is entirely Chinese MoE models of 300B to 2.8T total parameters; the only US frontier-ish open releases are NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Super/Ultra and the Aug-2025 gpt-oss-120b, and Meta has stopped shipping open weights.

### Inferences
- "Striking distance" (≤ ~10 AA points from the closed frontier, or top-10 Arena) in Sept 2026 means: Kimi K3 (2.8T), GLM-5.3 (744B), Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, GLM-5.3-Flash (320B), DeepSeek V4.1-Flash (552B), DeepSeek V4-Pro (1.6T). A second tier 5–10 points further back includes DeepSeek V4-Flash (284B), MiniMax M3 (428B), Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B), Hy3 (295B), Qwen3.5-397B, Kimi K2.7 (1T), Mistral Large 3 (675B).
- Every model in the top tier is a sparse MoE with 13–104B active parameters; there is no frontier-tier dense open model. Dense open models top out at 27–128B (Qwen3.8-27B, Gemma 4 31B, Mistral Medium 3.5 128B).
- The US open ecosystem has effectively narrowed to NVIDIA (Nemotron), OpenAI (gpt-oss, 2025), Ai2 (OLMo), Arcee, and Google (Gemma). Meta's exit removes the historic anchor for large US open weights.

## KQ2. Which of those fit in 128 GB at 4-bit with ≥32K context? Which need 256 GB (2 Sparks) or a 512 GB Mac Studio?

### Takeaway
At 4-bit, only the ~100–290B-total class fits a single 128 GB Spark with usable context (gpt-oss-120b, Nemotron 3 Super, Qwen3.5-122B, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, MiniMax M2.x at ~3–4 bpw, and — barely — DeepSeek V4-Flash at 110 GiB); the 300–430B class (GLM-5.3-Flash, Hy3, Qwen3.5-397B, MiniMax M3, Trinity) needs 2 Sparks; GLM-5.3, Nemotron 3 Ultra and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash need 3–4 Sparks (or ~2–2.5 bpw hacks on 2); and Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-Max and DeepSeek V4-Pro exceed even a 512 GB Mac Studio at any published quant.

- DGX Spark (GB10): 128 GB unified LPDDR5x, 273 GB/s, 240 W, ~$4,700 per unit; 2-unit cluster ~$9,400; 4 units ~$18,800. — [noze.it, Aug 6 2026](https://www.noze.it/en/insights/dgx-spark-local-models-august-2026/)

## KQ3. Is the "frontier open model" size trend going up, down, or bifurcating, and what does that imply for a 128 GB purchase in late 2026?

### Takeaway
Bifurcating: the flagship open models roughly tripled in size in 12 months (DeepSeek 671B → 1.6T, Kimi 1T → 2.8T, Qwen 397B → 2.4T) and now sit far beyond 256 GB, while every major lab simultaneously ships a "Flash/Super/Next" sibling at 120–550B total with 6–23B active that lands within a few AA points of its flagship and is explicitly targeted at 1–2 Spark-class boxes. A 128 GB box in late 2026 buys the second tier (AA ~36–42, roughly 11–17 points behind the closed frontier), and the second tier's total-parameter count is itself creeping up (284B → 320B → 552B for the Flash models), pushing the sweet spot toward 256 GB.

### Inferences
- Trend is clearly up at the top (flagships now 1.6–2.8T; 4-bit ≥ 0.9 TB) and simultaneously flat-to-slightly-up in the "efficient flagship" tier (120–550B). Nothing in the top tier will fit 128 GB, 256 GB, or 512 GB in 2027 short of ~1-bit quantization.
- Because labs now train natively in FP4 (Kimi K3 MXFP4, Nemotron NVFP4, DeepSeek FP4 experts), the "4-bit" footprint IS the official footprint — there is no further lossless headroom below it, so the usual "wait for a good Q3/Q2" fallback yields larger quality loss than it did for BF16-native models.
- Practical implication for a 128 GB purchase in late 2026: it will run AA-36–42-class models (Nemotron Super, Qwen3.5-122B, DeepSeek V4-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, gpt-oss-120b) at interactive speeds, and probably their 2027 successors if labs keep the ~120–290B "Flash/Super/Next" tier alive (which every major Chinese lab plus NVIDIA is doing). It will NOT run the AA-42–45 tier (GLM-5.3-Flash and up) with usable context; that tier requires 256 GB today and the "Flash" models are growing (284B → 552B), so 256 GB is the safer floor for "one step below the open frontier" through 2027.
- The 2-Spark cluster's bandwidth (2 × 273 GB/s with TP2) also matters: a second Spark nearly doubles decode speed for models that already fit on one (DeepSeek V4-Flash: 26 tok/s on one → 46–82 tok/s on two).

### Gaps
- No lab has published a roadmap stating that "Flash/Super"-class models will continue to be sized for ≤256 GB; the inference that this tier persists is based on the pattern of 2026 releases only.
- No source quantified quality loss for the 2–2.5 bpw hybrid quants (NVFP4 + AQLM 2-bit) used to squeeze GLM-5.2 into 3 Sparks; "usable" is asserted by hobbyist posts, not benchmarked.

## KQ4. What decode and prefill speed can a user realistically expect on Spark for the best models that fit?

### Takeaway
Measured single-stream decode on one Spark is ~60 tok/s for gpt-oss-120b (5.1B active, MXFP4), ~44 tok/s for Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B Q8, 81–120 tok/s for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B NVFP4 (with MTP spec decode), 33 tok/s for Qwen3.6-27B dense NVFP4, and 26 tok/s for DeepSeek V4-Flash (13B active) at 4-bit; on two Sparks (TP2) DeepSeek V4-Flash reaches 46–82 tok/s and GLM-5.3-Flash 29–32 tok/s prose / 41–70 tok/s with speculative decoding. - Dense ≥100B models are the worst fit for Spark: Mistral Medium 3.5 / Devstral 2 at 4-bit move ~65–70 GB per token → ~4 tok/s ceiling; Mistral ships an EAGLE head, which could roughly double that but still leaves it below the ~20 tok/s chat threshold.
- Speculative decoding (MTP heads, DFlash2, NEXTN, DSpark) is now the dominant lever on Spark: it lifts GLM-5.3-Flash from ~30 to ~70 tok/s on structured output and Qwen3.6-35B from ~81 to ~120 tok/s. Models that ship MTP heads (DeepSeek V4, GLM-5.3-Flash, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Nemotron 3, Hy3, Mistral Medium 3.5) should be preferred for Spark.
- Prefill scales with compute rather than bandwidth: expect ~1–2.5K tok/s at short depth and roughly half that at 32K+ depth for 100–300B MoEs; a 100K-token agentic context therefore costs ~40–100 s of prefill on 1–2 Sparks unless prefix caching is used.

- Measured numbers vary 20–40% across posts for the same model due to engine, quant, spec-decode and context depth; the WeZZard bench notes an earlier run was invalidated by a GPU stuck at 29% clock, illustrating how fragile hobbyist measurements are.

## KQ5. How do MoE active-parameter counts translate to interactive usability (≥20 tok/s chat, agentic coding), and what is expected in Q4 2026 / early 2027?

### Inferences
- Usability bands on one Spark at 4-bit (single stream, no speculative decoding): ≤6B active (gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.x-35B-A3B, Gemma 4 26B-A4B, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next) → 45–120 tok/s, excellent for chat and agent loops; 10–13B active (MiniMax M2.x, Nemotron Super, Qwen3.5-122B, DeepSeek V4-Flash) → 25–40 tok/s, good chat, acceptable agentic; 17–23B active (Qwen3.5-397B, GLM-5.3-Flash, Hy3, MiniMax M3) → 20–30 tok/s but these don't fit one Spark anyway; 40–55B active (GLM-5.3, Nemotron Ultra, DeepSeek V4-Pro) → ~9–13 tok/s per node, below the chat threshold without TP across 3–4 nodes; ≥95B active (Kimi K3, Qwen3.8-Max) → ~5 tok/s per node, impractical.
- For agentic coding (long contexts, many tool-call turns, heavy prefill), the practical floor is a 2-Spark cluster with a ≤18B-active model plus MTP/DFlash speculative decoding and prefix caching; measured results (22–42 tok/s at 4–33K agentic context) sit at or just above the 20 tok/s comfort line.
- Given Qwen4's architecture preview is a 125B-A6B model with a 51B n-gram table, at least one Qwen4 family member will very likely target 1-Spark class hardware; the Qwen3.8-Max precedent (2.4T) suggests the Qwen4 flagship will not.
