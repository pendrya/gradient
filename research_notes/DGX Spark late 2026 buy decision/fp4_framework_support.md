# FP4 (NVFP4 / MXFP4) inference & training support on DGX Spark (GB10, sm_121) — state as of 2026-09-20

Scope note: everything below is dated. "Today" = 2026-09-20. Claims sourced only from a search-result snippet (not a fetched page) are marked **[snippet]**. Anything I could not verify is in Gaps.

## Background: the FP4 format landscape and which hardware natively runs it

### Takeaway
NVFP4 (E2M1 values, FP8-E4M3 scale per 16 elements + a per-tensor FP32 scale) is measurably more accurate than OCP MXFP4 (E2M1, power-of-two E8M0 scale per 32 elements); both run on the same Blackwell FP4 tensor-core path, but only MXFP4 is portable to AMD MI355X. GB10 does have real FP4 tensor cores (measured ~511 TFLOPS dense / ~1 PFLOP 2:4-sparse), but it is a different ISA target (sm_121, Ampere-style `mma.sync`) from datacenter Blackwell (sm_100, `tcgen05`/TMEM), which is the root cause of most of the software gaps.

### Cited Findings
- NVFP4 uses 16-element blocks (vs 32 for MXFP4) and an FP8-E4M3 block scale instead of E8M0 power-of-two scales; NVIDIA states this "significantly reduc[es] quantization errors" — [NVIDIA: Introducing NVFP4](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/introducing-nvfp4-for-efficient-and-accurate-low-precision-inference/)
- In NVIDIA's pretraining paper, MXFP4 shows ~2.5% relative loss error vs ~1.5% for NVFP4, and MXFP4 needs ~36% more tokens to match NVFP4 loss — [Pretraining LLMs with NVFP4, arXiv 2509.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149)
- An academic 2026 paper reports the end-to-end accuracy gap between plain MXFP4 and NVFP4 is ~10% on average, reducible to <1% with Overflow-Aware Scaling / Macro Block Scaling software techniques — [arXiv 2603.08713 (MXFP4 error reduction)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08713v1) **[snippet]**
- Layer-wise sensitivity study (Qwen2.5 0.5B/7B/14B, March 2026): MLP projections are the most FP4-sensitive layers; early blocks can be highly sensitive "particularly under MXFP4" — [arXiv 2603.08747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08747)
- Hardware support table (June 13, 2026): B200/B300 (sm_100) NVFP4+MXFP4; RTX 5090 and RTX PRO 6000 NVFP4+MXFP4; AMD MI355X MXFP4 only (MFMA, ROCm 7.x), no NVFP4 — [Spheron NVFP4 vs MXFP4 guide](https://www.spheron.network/blog/nvfp4-vs-mxfp4-gpu-cloud-4bit-quantization-guide/) (note: that table mislabels RTX 5090/PRO 6000 as SM100; they are SM120)
- AMD serves NVFP4 checkpoints on MI350X/MI355X only by online re-quantizing NVFP4 → Quark MXFP4 — [AMD ROCm blog](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/nvfp4-to-mxfp4/README.html)
- GB10 spec: 48 SMs, 6,144 CUDA cores, 128 GB LPDDR5X at 273 GB/s, "1 PFLOP sparse FP4 (~500 TFLOPS dense)" — [Tom's Hardware DGX Spark review p.2](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-dgx-spark-review/2) **[snippet]**
- Measured on GB10 with the open-source `nvfp4bench` tool (June 17, 2026): ~511 TFLOPS dense NVFP4 and ~1014–1022 TFLOPS 2:4 sparse (102% of spec), but only via the packed `mxf4nvf4` instruction; the byte-padded `mxf8f6f4` path gives exactly half. Independent users on Asus/Lenovo/HP GB10 boxes reproduced 97–101% of spec — [NVIDIA forum: GB10 really does hit ~1 PFLOP NVFP4](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/gb10-really-does-hit-1-pflop-nvfp4-2-4-sparse-measured-with-an-open-source-tool-to-reproduce-it/373618)
- Same thread: real inference kernels are far below peak; a CUTLASS NVFP4 GEMM peaks ~375 TFLOPS on GB10, and decode "sits way down on the memory-bound side of the roofline" at ~0.1% of peak compute — [same forum thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/gb10-really-does-hit-1-pflop-nvfp4-2-4-sparse-measured-with-an-open-source-tool-to-reproduce-it/373618)
- Architecture divergence (Feb 19, 2026): SM121 lacks TMEM, `tcgen05`, WGMMA, SM clusters/multicast; has 128 KB shared memory (vs 228 KB on SM100) and 48 warps/SM (vs 64); tensor ops use "extended `mma.sync`," so a 64x64 tile that SM100 does in one op needs 32 `mma.sync` calls; "FlashMLA, FlashInfer, and FlashAttention all fail on SM121" when they target WGMMA/tcgen05 — [Backend.AI: Is DGX Spark actually Blackwell?](https://www.backend.ai/blog/2026-02-is-dgx-spark-actually-a-blackwell)
- Rubin (next gen) tensor cores add UE5M3 block-scale support on top of Blackwell's UE4M3/UE8M0, and double FP4/FP8 throughput per SM by doubling the k dimension; BF16/TF32 do not get the doubling — [SemiAnalysis: Vera Rubin extreme co-design](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/vera-rubin-extreme-co-design-an-evolution) **[snippet]**
- Earlier confusion: an April 2026 benchmark blog claimed SM121 lacks the `cvt.rn.satfinite.e2m1x2` PTX instruction; NVIDIA later clarified (2026-04-30 note) the instruction exists but requires the `sm_121a` target rather than `sm_121` — [ai-muninn: NVFP4 is a trap on GB10 (self-corrected)](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-nvfp4-trap-gb10-fp8-wins)

### Inferences
- The recurring GB10 story is not "no FP4 hardware" but "kernels compiled for sm_100/sm_120 or for plain sm_121 instead of sm_121a silently fall back." Anything that needs `tcgen05`/TMEM (most high-performance datacenter Blackwell kernels) can never run on GB10; it needs `mma.sync`-family kernels like those for RTX 50 (sm_120), with an extra `a` feature-flag compile.
- Apple silicon and AMD Strix Halo have no FP4 tensor path (no source found that claims otherwise; see Gaps), so the "FP4 native" advantage is unique to Blackwell-family devices in the ~128 GB unified-memory class.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source explicitly stating Apple silicon or Strix Halo lack FP4 tensor acceleration; the Spheron table simply omits them. Treat as very likely true but unverified here.
- No source enumerates exactly which CUTLASS features are missing on sm_121 vs sm_120 beyond the Backend.AI summary (which groups SM12x together).

## KQ1: Is NVFP4 first-class on GB10 in vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM and llama.cpp as of Sept 2026?

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 NVFP4 on GB10 is "works, usable, and improving" but not first-class: vLLM has native sm_121 NVFP4 GEMMs (PR #40082, May 2026) but still ships kernel-selection bugs (Sept 2026, #55405 open); SGLang runs NVFP4 on Spark with a Qwen3.8-specific PR and heavy community recipes; TRT-LLM's Spark playbook lists many NVFP4 models but single-stream speed lags llama.cpp; llama.cpp has native Blackwell NVFP4 (merged April 28, 2026, benchmarked on GB10) and native MXFP4 (merged Dec 24, 2025, sm_121 targeted). Ollama (Linux) and LM Studio do not deliver NVFP4 on Spark; bitsandbytes does not support NVFP4 at all.

### Cited Findings

- NVIDIA staff PSA (johnny_nv, March 30, 2026): NVFP4 on Spark needs FlashInfer including PR #2913, initially via a fork; "Spark uses SM 12.1a" — [NVIDIA forum PSA: State of FP4/NVFP4 in vLLM](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/psa-state-of-fp4-nvfp4-support-for-dgx-spark-in-vllm/353069/149)

### Inferences
- The practical hierarchy on a single Spark today: llama.cpp (GGUF Q4/MXFP4/NVFP4, best single-stream and least fuss) → vLLM nightly/NGC with NVFP4 (best when you need MTP/spec-decode, batch, or a model only published in NVFP4) → SGLang (works for specific models with recipes) → TRT-LLM (playbook exists; least community traction for single-user). Ollama/LM Studio are effectively GGUF-only on Spark.
- "Support" for GB10 tends to arrive 2–6 months after B200 and 1–3 months after RTX 50 (sm_120), typically through community forks/patched images first, then upstream.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative NVIDIA statement lists which vLLM/FlashInfer NVFP4 kernels are validated on sm_121a; forum staff comments are the closest.
- I did not find whether llama.cpp's NVFP4 W4A4 tensor-core path is enabled by default on GB10 builds or needs `121a` flags like the MXFP4 path (likely needs `-DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=121a`, unverified).
- No confirmation of Ollama Linux NVFP4 status after ~April 2026.

## KQ2: How does NVFP4 accuracy compare with Q4_K_M/IQ4 GGUF, AWQ/GPTQ INT4, and MXFP4 on the same models?

### Takeaway
NVFP4 (especially with QAD/Unsloth-style mixed precision) is within noise of FP8/BF16 on NVIDIA's and Unsloth's benchmarks and is clearly better than plain MXFP4; against GGUF Q4_K_M the only apples-to-apples numbers I found (llama.cpp PR #22196 on Nemotron 3 Super) show NVFP4 W4A4 slightly worse in perplexity (4.658 vs 4.624), and community quantizers argue EXL3/Q4_K give better quality-per-bit. Independent head-to-head suites are still thin.

### Cited Findings
- Unsloth Dynamic NVFP4 Qwen3.6-27B (MMLU-Pro/GPQA/AIME25): Unsloth 86.25/86.34/93.12; NVIDIA NVFP4 85.96/86.87/93.12; FP8 86.11/86.87/93.75 — [Unsloth NVFP4 docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/nvfp4)
- NVIDIA DeepSeek-V4-Flash-NVFP4 (ModelOpt v0.44, May 28, 2026) vs original: GPQA Diamond 0.894→0.891, AA-LCR 0.658→0.655, τ²-Bench Telecom 0.943→0.942, SciCode 0.481→0.481, IFBench 0.788→0.795 — [HF nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-NVFP4)
- llama.cpp on DGX Spark, Nemotron 3 Super 120B perplexity: Q4_K 4.6237±0.028; NVFP4 with Q8 activations (fallback) 4.6283; NVFP4 W4A4 tensor-core path 4.6577; maintainers acknowledge 4-bit activation quantization degrades quality — [llama.cpp PR #22196](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22196)

- eugr (Spark forum, Nov 2025) recommended FP8, AWQ and INT4-AutoRound over NVFP4 at that time for Spark — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/trt-llm-for-inference-with-nvfp4-safetensors-slower-than-lm-studio-gguf-on-the-spark/348636)

### Inferences
- Official NVFP4 checkpoints (ModelOpt PTQ with sensitive layers kept in FP8/BF16) are at FP8 quality on task benchmarks; the quality question is W4A4 (activations also 4-bit), which is where GGUF Q4_K (weights-only, 8-bit activations) retains an edge. On a bandwidth-bound single-user box that edge costs nothing.
- The mixed-precision recipes (Unsloth Dynamic, ModelOpt AutoQuantize) make "NVFP4" checkpoints larger than pure 4-bit; e.g. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next NVFP4 is 97.7 GiB vs GGUF q4 111.8 GiB **[snippet, evanwtf/local-llm #299]**.

### Gaps
- No published suite comparing NVFP4 vs Q4_K_M vs IQ4_XS vs AWQ/GPTQ INT4 on identical models with KLD/task scores; only scattered numbers above.
- No numbers found for MXFP4-vs-NVFP4 of the same model on GB10 in llama.cpp.

## KQ3: Does FP4 speed up decode on a 273 GB/s device, or only prefill/compute?

### Takeaway
On GB10, FP4's decode benefit comes almost entirely from smaller weights (bytes moved per token), not from FP4 tensor cores; native FP4 kernels raise prefill 20–60% but leave token generation essentially unchanged in every measurement found. Observed single-stream decode on one Spark: ~12 tok/s (120B-A12B), ~23–24 tok/s (Nemotron Super vLLM), ~41–50 tok/s (30B-A3B / MTP-assisted), ~59–69 tok/s (gpt-oss-120b MXFP4), with decode consuming only ~155–160 GB/s of the 273 GB/s.

### Cited Findings
- llama.cpp NVFP4 tensor-core path on Spark (Nemotron 3 Super): pp2048 +21% (506→611), tg128 unchanged (12.0 vs 11.9 tok/s) — [llama.cpp PR #22196](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/22196)
- llama.cpp MXFP4 native path on Spark (gpt-oss-120B): pp2048 1,937→2,438 tok/s; tg 59.05→57.81 tok/s (no gain) — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/llama-cpp-experimental-native-mxfp4-support-for-blackwell-pr/355639)
- On RTX 5090 native NVFP4: prefill +43–68%, token generation ~0% — [InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/fp4-inference-llamacpp-nvfp4-mxfp4/)
- vLLM Qwen3.8-Flash-Next NVFP4 on Spark: decode measures ~154–158 GB/s ("about 60% of spec"); author: compression efficiency, not arithmetic speed, drives NVFP4 gains; 41.7 tok/s with MTP vs 17 tok/s without; llama.cpp same model 23.4 tok/s — [ai-muninn recipe (Sept 6, 2026)](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/qwen38-flash-next-nvfp4-dgx-spark-vllm-recipe)
- vLLM 0.19.1 on Spark, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: FP8 53.8 tok/s vs NVFP4 (Marlin fallback) 40.8 tok/s — FP8 32% faster (April 21, 2026) — [ai-muninn NVFP4 trap](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-nvfp4-trap-gb10-fp8-wins); a 30-line Triton FP8 bypass then took NVFP4 to 47.6 tok/s (+17%) — [ai-muninn Triton patch](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-nvfp4-fp8-triton-patch) **[snippet]**
- vLLM Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-NVFP4 on one Spark: 22.7–23.7 tok/s decode, up to 1,884 tok/s prefill; "decode performance remains bounded by active parameter count and kernel selection rather than raw bandwidth" — [vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-06-01-vllm-dgx-spark)
- Gemma-4-26B-A4B NVFP4 on Spark: 158 tok/s single-stream, 257 aggregate — [Medium: Benchmarking Gemma-4-26B on Spark](https://medium.com/@james-tang/benchmarking-gemma-4-26b-a4b-on-the-dgx-spark-dc8245292095) **[snippet]**; another review: 1,723 tok/s prefill but 38 tok/s decode — [IntuitionLabs review](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/nvidia-dgx-spark-review) **[snippet]**
- gpt-oss-120b MXFP4 on one Spark via patched vLLM + SM121 CUTLASS MXFP4 kernels: 68.9 tok/s single-stream, 300 tok/s at 30 users — [luka-loehr/gptoss-spark](https://github.com/luka-loehr/gptoss-spark) **[snippet]**; ~59–60 tok/s in llama.cpp and vLLM MXFP4 — [ai-muninn part 2](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/part2-gpt-oss-120b-serve-script) **[snippet]**
- Concurrency scales: 695 tok/s aggregate at 256 streams on one Spark — [Dendro Logic concurrency benchmark](https://dendro-logic.com/engineering/nvidia-dgx-spark-concurrency-benchmark/) **[snippet]**
- Video model: NVFP4 shrank a video model 33% on Spark "with zero speed gain" — [ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-sulphur-nvfp4-video) **[snippet]**
- NVIDIA (Jan 5, 2026): FP8→NVFP4 plus Eagle-3 spec decoding gives 2.6x for Qwen-235B on Spark (TRT-LLM); llama.cpp MoE +35% avg; NVFP4 40% memory reduction — [NVIDIA blog: new software optimizations for DGX Spark](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/new-software-and-model-optimizations-supercharge-nvidia-dgx-spark)
- Roofline: decode at ~0.1% of GB10's FP4 peak; CUTLASS NVFP4 GEMM ~375 TFLOPS max on GB10 — [NVIDIA forum 1 PFLOP thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/gb10-really-does-hit-1-pflop-nvfp4-2-4-sparse-measured-with-an-open-source-tool-to-reproduce-it/373618)

### Inferences
- For a one-or-two-Spark user the value of FP4 is (a) fitting bigger models (200B-class MoE in ~100 GB) and (b) prefill/TTFT and batch throughput; single-stream decode gains vs Q4 GGUF are ~0 unless combined with speculative decoding (MTP/Eagle/DFlash), which is where vLLM/SGLang NVFP4 stacks pull ahead of llama.cpp (41.7 vs 23.4 tok/s on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next).
- Where a W4A4 kernel is *missing* (Marlin dequant fallback), NVFP4 can be slower than FP8 on GB10 despite half the bytes, because the dequant is compute-heavy on a 48-SM GPU.

### Gaps
- No controlled same-model, same-engine NVFP4-vs-Q4_K_M decode comparison at identical context on GB10 beyond the PR #22196 table (which compares branches, not formats).

## KQ4: Is DGX Spark at risk of being left behind by FP4 developments?

### Takeaway
Moderate risk, mostly software-lag rather than hard obsolescence: GB10 has real NVFP4/MXFP4 tensor cores and the FP4 checkpoint ecosystem (DeepSeek-V4 native MXFP4, NVIDIA NVFP4 releases of DeepSeek/Kimi/Qwen/Nemotron/GLM) is converging on formats GB10 can run; but new kernels routinely target sm_100 first, sm_121 needs `sm_121a` builds, and Rubin adds a UE5M3 scale format Blackwell cannot execute natively.

### Cited Findings
- Frontier open-weight checkpoints published in NVFP4 by NVIDIA: DeepSeek-V4-Pro (1.6T/49B active), V4-Pro-0813, V4-Flash (284B/13B), V4-Flash-0731, V4.1-Flash, V3.2 — [HF search results](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Pro-NVFP4) **[snippet]**, [nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-NVFP4); Kimi-K2.5-NVFP4 (1T, 591 GB, serve with TP=4) — [HF nvidia/Kimi-K2.5-NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Kimi-K2.5-NVFP4) **[snippet]**, [Spark forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nvidia-releases-kimi-k2-5-nvfp4-1t-591gb/359810) **[snippet]**; Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, Qwen3.8-27B — [HF nvidia/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-NVFP4](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-NVFP4) **[snippet]**; Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B-NVFP4, Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B-NVFP4 (made with Model Optimizer) — [NVIDIA blog on Nemotron 3 Ultra NVFP4](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/creating-the-nvidia-nemotron-3-ultra-nvfp4-checkpoint-with-nvidia-model-optimizer/) **[snippet]**; Llama-4-Scout-17B NVFP4 and Qwen3-235B in the Spark TRT-LLM playbook — [build.nvidia.com](https://build.nvidia.com/spark/trt-llm/overview); GLM-4.7-NVFP4 served with TRT-LLM on 2x Spark — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/glm-4-7-nvfp4-not-flash-served-with-trt-llm-on-2x-dgx-spark/358551) **[snippet]**; GLM-5.3-Flash NVFP4 (community LibertAIDAI) on 2x/4x Spark — [tonyd2wild 4x repo](https://github.com/tonyd2wild/GLM-5.3-Flash-NVFP4-1M-KV-4x-DGX-Spark) **[snippet]**

### Inferences
- The trend toward 4-bit-as-canonical (DeepSeek-V4 experts in MXFP4, gpt-oss MXFP4, Nemotron trained in NVFP4) favours GB10 over Apple/Strix Halo, since those checkpoints run in their native format on Blackwell tensor cores; on non-FP4 hardware they must be dequantized or re-quantized.
- The main "left behind" risk is upstream kernel authors prioritising sm_100 (tcgen05) and sm_120, with sm_121a as a trailing target. Because GB10 shares the `mma.sync` programming model with RTX 50, it generally inherits RTX-50 kernels with a recompile; features that fundamentally require TMEM/clusters (e.g., FlashMLA-class attention) will never arrive.
- Rubin-era UE5M3-scaled checkpoints would not be natively executable on Blackwell; whether model publishers adopt UE5M3 as canonical is unknown.

### Gaps
- No NVIDIA roadmap statement on continued sm_121 kernel investment (FlashInfer/CUTLASS) was found.
- Could not verify Llama 4 (beyond Scout in TRT-LLM playbook), Mistral, GLM-5 (non-Flash) or Qwen3.5 dense NVFP4 checkpoints from NVIDIA directly; community quants exist for some.
- No source on whether Nemotron 3 will ship NVFP4 as *canonical* (Super ships FP8 canonical; NVFP4 is a ModelOpt PTQ artifact).

## KQ5: Status of FP4 training / fine-tuning (QAT, NVFP4 pretraining recipes) on a single GB10

### Takeaway
NVFP4 compute training exists on GB10 through Transformer Engine (with QLoRA-style LoRA) and QAT via TorchAO/Unsloth and ReQAT-type recipes, but it is early: no throughput benchmarks on Spark, the recommended default for a single Spark is BF16 fine-tuning unless a tested NVFP4 recipe exists, and NVIDIA's own NVFP4 pretraining recipe is a multi-GPU datacenter workflow.

### Cited Findings
- Community project "dgx-spark-finetune-llm": LoRA fine-tuning on GB10 with three backends — bitsandbytes FP4 (~45 GB for a 3B model), Transformer Engine NVFP4 ("Native Blackwell FP4 training via Transformer Engine," ~41 GB), TE MXFP8 (~50 GB); uses TE 2.9+, nvidia-modelopt, PEFT; "work in progress," no benchmarks — [waybarrios/dgx-spark-finetune-llm](https://github.com/waybarrios/dgx-spark-finetune-llm)
- Transformer Engine supports FP8 and FP4 training on Blackwell — [NVIDIA/TransformerEngine](https://github.com/NVIDIA/TransformerEngine/) **[snippet]**
- Kubesimplify Spark quantization guide: "for a single DGX Spark, starting training and fine-tuning in BF16 is recommended unless the exact project gives a tested NVFP4 recipe" — [Kubesimplify Day 4](https://blog.kubesimplify.com/day-4-quantization-demystified-bf16-fp8-nvfp4-mxfp4-int4-gguf-and-why-it-all-matters) **[snippet]**
- NVIDIA Spark playbooks include an NVFP4 quantization (PTQ with Model Optimizer) playbook and distributed FSDP+LoRA fine-tuning of up to 70B across two Sparks — [NVIDIA/dgx-spark-playbooks nvfp4-quantization](https://github.com/NVIDIA/dgx-spark-playbooks/blob/main/nvidia/station-nvfp4-quantization/README.md) **[snippet]**; [NVIDIA blog Jan 5, 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/new-software-and-model-optimizations-supercharge-nvidia-dgx-spark)
- ReQAT (ICML 2026, submitted June 14, 2026): reasoning-centric W4A4KV4 FP4 QAT that "recovers but surpasses BF16 fine-tuning accuracy"; evaluated on DGX Spark and B200 with up to 3.9x (Spark) / 3.1x (B200) throughput speedup — [arXiv 2606.15682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15682)
- Unsloth + TorchAO QAT recovers "up to 70%" of quantization loss (1–3% gains on GPQA/MMLU-Pro) — [Unsloth changelog](https://unsloth.ai/docs/new/changelog) **[snippet]**; Unsloth supports Blackwell RTX 50/PRO 6000/B200 for fine-tuning — [Unsloth Blackwell docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/blog/fine-tuning-llms-with-blackwell-rtx-50-series-and-unsloth) **[snippet]**; NVIDIA blog on training with Unsloth on a Blackwell desktop — [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/train-an-llm-on-an-nvidia-blackwell-desktop-with-unsloth-and-scale-it/) **[snippet]**
- Kaitchup: Spark "makes sense for fine-tuning and small-scale training, especially for models under ~8B parameters" — [Kaitchup: DGX Spark for fine-tuning](https://kaitchup.substack.com/p/dgx-spark-use-it-for-fine-tuning) **[snippet]**
- NVFP4 pretraining recipe needs 2D block scaling, RHT, stochastic rounding, selective BF16 layers; validated on GB200/GB300 — [arXiv 2509.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149)

### Inferences
- On one Spark, FP4 for training is primarily a memory lever (TE NVFP4 QLoRA at ~41 GB for a 3B model suggests overhead is high in current WIP code) rather than a speed lever; the 273 GB/s bandwidth and 48 SMs bound step time.
- The NVFP4 pretraining recipe is the same one Nemotron 3 used; nothing indicates it has been packaged for single-GB10 use, and its 2–3x gains are relative to FP8 on GB200/GB300-class parts.

### Gaps
- No tok/s or step-time benchmarks for TE NVFP4 or MXFP8 fine-tuning on GB10.
- No confirmation that Unsloth's QAT path runs on sm_121 specifically (documentation lists RTX 50/PRO/B200).
- No Model Optimizer QAT (as opposed to PTQ) walkthrough for DGX Spark found.
