# Open-source model-optimization projects and the DGX Spark (GB10) — state as of September 2026

Scope: Unsloth, llama.cpp/ggml, ik_llama.cpp, exllamav3, MLX (Apple comparator), vLLM/SGLang, NVIDIA playbooks, quant producers (bartowski, mradermacher, unsloth, ubergarm), TurboQuant/KV-cache compression, community hubs, and realistic fine-tuning on one Spark. Dates are given per claim; anything from SEO/aggregator sites is flagged as lower-confidence. Research date: 2026-09-20.

## Key Question 1: What has Unsloth shipped specifically for DGX Spark / Blackwell (GB10) in 2025–2026, and what do they say about its limits?

### Takeaway
Unsloth is the officially blessed fine-tuning stack for DGX Spark (NVIDIA playbook + co-authored NVIDIA blog posts, Oct–Dec 2025), and it has kept the Spark as a supported target through 2026: QLoRA of gpt-oss-120b in ~68 GB, RL/GRPO of gpt-oss-20b demonstrated live, Dynamic NVFP4 inference quants with a Spark-specific MoE backend flag, and an in-flight (Sept 2026, still open) PR adding two-Spark pipeline training and a `spark doctor` tool. Their stated limits: ~121.7 GiB usable, "pipeline parallel cannot speed up decode," models that fit one Spark run ~0.92x when split across two, and NVFP4 is inference-only — no FP4 training path is documented.

### Cited Findings
- Unsloth's DGX Spark tutorial: fine-tune LLMs up to ~200B parameters on a Spark; gpt-oss-120b QLoRA 4-bit uses ~68 GB of the 128 GB; gpt-oss-20b trained with RL (GRPO) on a Spark "to auto-win 2048" — after 1,000 steps / 4 hours it "greatly outperforms the original" (shown at OpenAI DevDay, Oct 2025). Install is via Docker (CUDA 13.0, PyTorch 25.09 container, Triton and xformers built from source "for blackwell support"). Only gpt-oss is demonstrated; no 5090 comparison, no FP4 training numbers. — [Unsloth docs: Fine-tuning LLMs with NVIDIA DGX Spark and Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/blog/fine-tuning-llms-with-nvidia-dgx-spark-and-unsloth)

### Inferences
- Unsloth treats Spark as first-class for *fine-tuning* (playbook, Docker image, NVIDIA co-marketing, dedicated two-node PR), but its Blackwell FP4 work is inference-only (Dynamic NVFP4 quants, exported after training) — there is no evidence of NVFP4/FP4 *training* on Spark from Unsloth as of Sept 2026.
- The "up to 200B" claim is a QLoRA/4-bit statement (gpt-oss-120b at ~68 GB); the NVIDIA "70B fine-tune" claim is the same class of QLoRA. Full fine-tuning on one Spark is implicitly limited to small models (Unsloth's own Spark docs never demonstrate full FT above the 3B–8B class).
- The still-open Sept 2026 PR shows Unsloth is actively engineering around Spark's realities (121.7 GiB usable, interconnect faults, slow provisioning), which is a strong signal of continued commitment rather than abandonment.

### Gaps
- No Unsloth-published tokens/sec training throughput on GB10 for QLoRA/LoRA; only the two-node DualPipeV number (2,149 tok/s single node, model unspecified in the fetched summary).
- Whether PR #10280 merges before the buy decision is unknown (open as of Sept 2026).
- No official Unsloth statement comparing Spark training speed to RTX 5090 or Mac; their 2x/70% claims are measured on an RTX 5090.

## Key Question 2: How well does llama.cpp perform on GB10 today versus at launch (Oct 2025), and what optimizations landed?

### Takeaway
Georgi Gerganov personally benchmarked and tuned llama.cpp on Spark from Oct 14, 2025 onward; the tracked gains through Feb 2026 came from CUDA scheduling changes, disabling mmap, kernel/driver fixes to load time, and NVIDIA's CES-2026 claim of an average 35% MoE uplift. The big 2026 steps are native NVFP4 MMQ kernels for Blackwell (merged Apr 28, 2026; ~21% faster prefill on Nemotron-3-Super-120B on a Spark) and native MTP speculative decoding (`--spec-type draft-mtp`), which roughly doubles or triples single-stream decode on Qwen3.6/3.8-27B on GB10 (13→28 tok/s; 11→36 tok/s). Raw bandwidth-bound decode (~60 tok/s on gpt-oss-120b MXFP4, ~2.7 tok/s on dense 70B FP8/Q4) has not fundamentally changed.

### Cited Findings
- Launch benchmarks (Oct 14, 2025, ggerganov, updated through Feb 5, 2026): gpt-oss-20b ~2,009 tok/s pp2048 / ~61 tok/s tg32; gpt-oss-120b ~1,957 pp / ~60 tg; Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B ~1,654 pp / ~44 tg; throughput drops 40–50% at 32K depth; optimization log: Oct 15 CUDA scheduling strategy change improved decode; Oct 26 "various improvements plus disabled mmap"; Nov 9 more; Feb 5, 2026 latest. Load time for a large model went from 1m44s → 49s (unified memory enabled) → 22s (NVIDIA 6.17.1 kernel). Framework Desktop (Strix Halo) had "similar performance"; AI MAX+ 395 prefill ~1,000 tok/s vs 1,900+ on Spark. — [llama.cpp Discussion #16578](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/16578); [ggerganov on X](https://x.com/ggerganov/status/1978106631884828843)

### Inferences
- The maintainer-level attention (ggerganov's own benchmark thread, NVIDIA engineers reviewing NVFP4 PRs, ggerganov replying in Spark threads in Aug 2026) makes Spark a first-class CUDA target in llama.cpp — but the gains are concentrated in prefill (NVFP4 tensor cores) and speculative decode (MTP), not in raw decode bandwidth.
- For a single user, MTP-capable models (Qwen3.6/3.8, GLM-5.x, DeepSeek-V4 with DSpark) are the only path to 30–45 tok/s on 27B-class dense models on a Spark; without MTP, 27B dense Q4 sits at ~11–13 tok/s.
- The 2x-Spark RPC path is a capacity play (fit 150–200 GB models at 8–14 tok/s), not a speed play.

### Gaps
- No single, dated "launch vs today" apples-to-apples table for the same model/quant on GB10 was found; NVIDIA's 2.5x is workload-cherry-picked and includes TRT-LLM.
- No direct measurement of CUDA-graphs-specific gains on GB10 was found (the Oct 15 "CUDA scheduling" entry is the closest).
- TurboQuant-style KV compression is not in upstream llama.cpp (see Q3), so KV memory on Spark still relies on q8_0/q4_0 cache types.

## Key Question 3: Which community quant formats give the best quality-per-GB for fitting ~200–400B MoE models into 128 GB or 256 GB (2 Sparks)?

### Takeaway
For one Spark (~110–120 GB usable for weights+KV), the working answers are Unsloth UD-IQ3_XXS-class GGUFs (DeepSeek-V4-Flash 284B at 103 GB), EXL3 at ~3.0 bpw (DeepSeek-V4-Flash at 99.5 GiB, 44–47 tok/s with DSpark, "Q4_K_M–Q5 GGUF quality"), and NVFP4 for ~120B MoE (Nemotron-3-Super-120B in vLLM). For 256 GB, UD-Q4_K_XL of DeepSeek-V4-Flash (155 GB), 320B GLM-5.3-Flash EXL3 at ~2.9 bpw, and 1–2-bit UD quants of 400B–700B models become possible at single-digit tok/s. Independent KLD benchmarks in 2026 show no single quant house dominates: bartowski, mradermacher and unsloth trade frontier positions, and ubergarm's ik_llama quants win at extreme compression.

### Cited Findings
- Unsloth Dynamic 2.0 GGUFs quantize every layer at a different bit-width; UD-IQ3_XXS of DeepSeek-V4-Flash (284B/13B active) is 103 GB and "fits on a 128GB RAM device"; UD-Q4_K_XL 155 GB; UD-Q8_K_XL 162 GB (lossless). — [Unsloth DeepSeek-V4](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/deepseek-v4); [unsloth/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-GGUF); [Unsloth Dynamic 2.0 collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/unsloth/unsloth-dynamic-20-quants)

### Inferences
- Quality-per-GB at 3 bpw: EXL3 (trellis) and UD-IQ3_XXS are the two practical formats for 280B-class MoE on one Spark; EXL3 currently has the best measured decode on GB10 (44–47 tok/s with DSpark) but a smaller/tinkerer ecosystem; UD GGUF has the broadest tooling and llama.cpp MTP/DSpark support.
- NVFP4 is the hardware-native path but is quality-costly at W4A4 (0.7% PPL in llama.cpp; "undeniably hurts") and is mostly used at ~120B MoE where it fits with room for KV; for 280B+ MoE on one Spark NVFP4 is too large (a 4-bit 284B model is ~155 GB).
- For 256 GB (2 Sparks), realistic frontier-class targets are DeepSeek-V4-Flash UD-Q4_K_XL / EXL3 ~4 bpw and GLM-5.3-Flash 320B at 2.9–3 bpw; 1.6T DeepSeek-V4-Pro is out of reach even at 2 bpw (~400 GB).
- KV-cache memory for long context on Spark still depends on q8_0/fp8 KV; TurboQuant is a fork-only feature in llama.cpp and is marginal (22% over Q4_0) with stability caveats.

### Gaps
- No apples-to-apples KLD comparison of EXL3 3 bpw vs UD-IQ3_XXS for the same 280B+ MoE was found; the "Q5-ish" EXL3 claim is a recipe author's impression.
- No published NVFP4-vs-GGUF quality comparison for DeepSeek-V4/GLM-5.3 on Spark.
- ik_llama.cpp's actual performance on GB10 is undocumented in its README; no Spark-specific ik_llama benchmarks were found (search results conflated it with croll83's fork).

## Key Question 4: What fine-tuning/RL workloads have people actually completed on a Spark, with what throughput, and what do they say about 273 GB/s and the Blackwell FP4 path for training?

### Takeaway
Completed workloads on one Spark include gpt-oss-120b QLoRA (~68 GB), gpt-oss-20b GRPO (1,000 steps in 4 h), Llama-3.3-70B QLoRA (NVIDIA: 5,079 tok/s FP4; The Register: ~20 min job vs 50+ min on Strix Halo), Gemma-3-4B LoRA (10–12 h per 3-epoch run on 10K pairs), FLUX Dreambooth LoRA at rank 256, and GR00T robotics training; NVIDIA's peak numbers are 82,739 tok/s full-FT Llama-3.2-3B BF16 and 53,657 tok/s LoRA Llama-3.1-8B. Users consistently say bandwidth limits *decode*, that training is compute-bound enough that Spark beats Strix Halo by 1.5–2.5x, and that memory fragmentation and arm64/CUDA-13 packaging are the real pain; the Blackwell FP4 path is used for QLoRA weight storage (NVIDIA's 70B QLoRA "FP4") and NVFP4 pretraining playbooks (Megatron Bridge), but no community reports of NVFP4 *training* on Spark were found.

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA published fine-tuning throughput for DGX Spark: Llama 3.2 3B full fine-tune BF16 82,739 tok/s; Llama 3.1 8B LoRA BF16 53,657 tok/s; Llama 3.3 70B QLoRA FP4 5,079 tok/s (author notes these likely use NVIDIA-optimized stacks, not plain PyTorch). — [AIXplore/rundatarun, Oct 26–28, 2025](https://ai.rundatarun.io/Practical+Applications/dgx-lab-benchmarks-vs-reality-day-4); [NVIDIA blog on Spark performance](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-nvidia-dgx-sparks-performance-enables-intensive-ai-tasks)
- Same author's hands-on: Gemma-3-4b-it LoRA (r=16, α=32) on 10,000 medical QA pairs, 3 epochs, bs=4, BF16 → 10–12 h per run; seven models trained successfully; GPU memory fragmentation caused system instability requiring a hard reboot after 7.5 h, mitigated with cache clearing every 50 steps, 2–3 h sessions, checkpoints every 200 steps; "15 hours of debugging" traced to FP16 inference inf/nan (not training). — [AIXplore](https://ai.rundatarun.io/Practical+Applications/dgx-lab-benchmarks-vs-reality-day-4)
- Unsloth on Spark: gpt-oss-120b QLoRA ~68 GB; gpt-oss-20b GRPO 1,000 steps in 4 h (~250 steps/h). — [Unsloth docs](https://unsloth.ai/docs/blog/fine-tuning-llms-with-nvidia-dgx-spark-and-unsloth); [Unsloth gpt-oss RL tutorial](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gpt-oss-how-to-run-and-fine-tune/gpt-oss-reinforcement-learning/tutorial-how-to-train-gpt-oss-with-rl)

### Inferences
- Realistic one-Spark fine-tuning envelope in Sept 2026: QLoRA/LoRA up to ~120B MoE or ~70B dense (with little KV/batch headroom; Unsloth's own two-node PR splits 70B); full fine-tuning practical only for ≤3B–8B; GRPO practical on 20B-class models (gpt-oss-20b at ~250 steps/h).
- Throughput vs RTX 5090: no reliable head-to-head training benchmark was found; the ~6.5x bandwidth gap and "5070-class" compute imply a 5090 is several-x faster on anything that fits in 32 GB, while Spark wins only when the model does not fit a 5090 (≥~30B QLoRA) or when long context/large batch needs the 128 GB pool.
- The Blackwell FP4 path matters for *inference* of what you trained (export to NVFP4 via Unsloth Studio) more than for training itself; NVFP4 training exists only in NVIDIA's Megatron Bridge playbook, not in the hobbyist stack.

### Gaps
- No Unsloth or community tokens/sec numbers for QLoRA on GB10 vs RTX 5090 vs Mac MLX with the same model/config (the 82,739/53,657/5,079 figures are NVIDIA-stack numbers with unstated software).
- No published report of GRPO on models larger than gpt-oss-20b on one Spark, nor of RL throughput with vLLM rollouts on GB10.
- No community report of NVFP4-precision (W4) fine-tuning on Spark.

## Key Question 5: Is the open-source optimizer community treating Spark as a first-class target, an afterthought, or something they recommend against (vs Strix Halo and Mac)?

### Takeaway
By Sept 2026 the CUDA-side projects (llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang, Unsloth, exllamav3 community) treat GB10 as a first-class target with dedicated Docker images, playbooks, leaderboards (Spark Arena) and a 500+-project awesome list; the criticism is about *value*, not support: enthusiasts on Level1Techs and reviewers call it "frustratingly slow" for agentic decode (~273 GB/s) and point to Strix Halo (cheaper, similar decode, weaker prefill/training) or Mac Studio M5 Max/Ultra (614 GB/s–1.2 TB/s, up to 512 GB, MLX) as the alternatives for inference-first buyers. Unsloth itself now ships AMD (Strix Halo) and Mac/MLX support, so the fine-tuning moat has narrowed but CUDA remains the path of least resistance.

### Inferences
- Verdict on stance: first-class for support (Unsloth, llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang, NVIDIA all ship Spark-specific artifacts and respond to Spark threads), but the enthusiast consensus is "buy it for capacity, CUDA compatibility and training/prefill — not for decode speed."
